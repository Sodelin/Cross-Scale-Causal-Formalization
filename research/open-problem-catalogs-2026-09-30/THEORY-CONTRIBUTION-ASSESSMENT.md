# What Nolan's theory contributes to the omnibus

Assessment: 30 September 2026. Nolan Downard supplied the illumination/shadow, missed-scope and generative-search proposal. Observation maps, compatible-model sets and the intervention design below are analytical interpretations developed by contributors. This assessment uses the existing [theory analysis](../nolan-scope-theory-2026-09-30/snapshots/theory-analysis.md), [research map](OMNIBUS-MAP.md), [prior-corpus scan](EXISTING-RESEARCH-SCAN.md) and three catalog source records; it is not an exhaustive priority search or a new theorem.

## The useful claim

**Your framework can guide this research. Its strongest contribution concerns discovering the question before solving it.** An assistant can execute a narrow representation correctly while overlooking a much more consequential target. Asking whether the answer captures the intended system, and whether its mechanism survives elsewhere, challenges that representation. Checking completed steps alone need not do this.

That distinguishes two failures: missing something necessary inside the application, and missing a useful extension or connection outside it. The NANUQ example illustrates the second: asking about all levels redirected attention toward whether the argument actually depended on level. That was a productive research judgment. The recorded result does not establish how often the same question would succeed, or isolate intuition, chance and prior learning as competing causes. It does demonstrate a useful operation worth transferring.

Your distinctive emphasis should remain: **generate a consequential alternative target and investigate what would make it true.** Replacing this with “document a strengthening attempt” would lose the mechanism and reward paperwork. Correctness verification remains necessary, but serves a different purpose.

## What is established, useful, or still a hypothesis

| Part of the proposal | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Observations can conceal different hidden mechanisms | Established identifiability issue. A response is exactly recoverable when observationally indistinguishable admitted systems agree on that response. The general decoder criterion is prior mathematics. |
| We can recover a useful response without reconstructing everything | Established decision-sufficiency principle; also directly relevant to Ye, Amin and Özdağlar's [COLT 2026 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/ye26a.html). Its remaining noisy-learning questions require substantive statistical work. |
| Local success can miss the intended outcome or a better target | A sound logical distinction. Its frequency in assistants and its causes require evidence. |
| Combine internal completeness, transferable structure and generative target selection | Useful conceptual and workflow synthesis. We have not established literature-wide originality for this combination. Its usefulness does not depend on being the first wording of its components. |
| A targeted generation intervention beats ordinary execution or auditing per unit compute | Testable research hypothesis. Compare held-out starting problems under matched budgets; assess the strongest correct, relevant result rather than document counts. |
| Reward seeking explains these failures | Not established by these interactions. Training incentives, task interpretation, familiar methods and resource constraints could produce similar behavior. No inference about dopamine, enjoyment or an accessible internal reward center is warranted. |

The framework does not yet give a universal optimal search procedure. A best candidate within a declared set can still miss a better candidate outside it. Proving a runner requires a proposal record also cannot prove that its proposals are meaningful.

## The refinement the shadow idea needs

A shadow is best treated as **a specified observation of a system**, with an explicit requested response. Two systems can cast the same shadow yet react differently to an intervention. Full observation of a lossy summary therefore need not recover topology, mechanism or the best action. More data can improve inference, leave the relevant ambiguity unchanged, or expose model misspecification. No universal percentage-observed threshold follows.

Two sets should remain separate: the **candidate questions an agent considers**, and the **models compatible with the evidence**. Better problem representation can expand the first. Better measurements can constrain the second. Calling both sets C would hide the theory's most important distinction. Likewise, “objective subset” and “subjective whole” are too strong: measurements have error and interpretation, while inferred claims can have rigorous evidence.

Hard versus soft science should become a property of a particular question, measurement and model, rather than a single ranking of disciplines. Psychological behavior can identify one response well while leaving its mechanism ambiguous; biological and physical measurements can do the same. Shared shadows support transfer only when the relevant assumptions and response are preserved. Similar vocabulary does not establish self-similarity.

This is a refinement of your proposal, not a replacement. It makes “illumination” actionable: **which missing distinction could change the answer?**

## What to retain and what to change

Retain the two scope questions, the shadow metaphor, and the demand that alternatives be generated rather than merely audited. Refine full illumination into query-specific identification and explicitly preserved transfer. Remove unsupported universal guarantees and causal claims about model motivation. Reuse established mathematics instead of building another foundation around elementary facts.

The defensible presentation is: **a framework for discovering stronger research targets by exposing omitted mechanisms, unnecessary restrictions and decision-relevant information.** The contribution becomes scientifically substantial through an exact domain result or an evaluated discovery intervention. The present framework provides their organizing rationale; it has not yet proved either.
